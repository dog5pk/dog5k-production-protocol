Why DPP exists

AI can produce convincing work faster than humans can verify it.

Plausibility is not proof. Confidence is not evidence. A polished response can still contain missing logic, false claims, untested code, invented citations, broken links, or a quietly abandoned objective.

DPP establishes a standard for preventing those failures from being disguised as completion.

The source whitepaper defines the design rationale, production lifecycle, compliance model, limitations, benchmark methodology, and research agenda.

Core obligations

What DPP requires

01

Reality Wins

Correctness outranks appearance. Function outranks presentation.

02

Finish the Work

Do not convert implementation into explanation or partial progress into completion.

03

Truth Over Confidence

Confidence must never exceed the available evidence.

04

Evidence First

Claims should be supported according to their importance and risk.

05

Respect Constraints

Explicit constraints remain binding until explicitly changed.

06

Build for Inspection

Important decisions, claims, tests, inputs, and outputs should be reviewable.

Evidence, not advertising

DPP is designed to be tested.

The benchmark protocol compares identical tasks with and without DPP, preserves complete prompts and outputs, scores nine dimensions, and records critical failures separately from total scores.

View the Benchmark Protocol

Evaluation dimensions

  • Correctness
  • Completeness
  • Evidence quality
  • Constraint compliance
  • Inspectability
  • Uncertainty honesty
  • Maintainability
  • Efficiency
  • Professional judgment
